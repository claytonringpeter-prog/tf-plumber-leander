Water Heater Repair in Leander, TX

Water heater fault checks and repairs for cold faucets, leaks, and failing parts.

Call (512) 799-9083 Ask a question

A water heater can leave every faucet cold without putting a drop on the floor. A leaking tank or relief valve can show up from the hallway as water spreading from a utility closet, rusty water in a drain pan, or a wet floor by the heater.

We handle both no-hot-water faults and water escaping from the heater. An electric unit may have a failed element or thermostat. A gas unit may have an ignition, burner, vent, or control problem.

A dry loss of hot water can sometimes wait for a planned repair. Water around the tank, water from the relief valve, or a gas odor should not wait because the leak can damage flooring and framing, and a gas problem needs immediate attention.

No hot water or popping

Customers call us because the shower turns cold halfway through, the kitchen faucet never gets hot, or the water stays lukewarm. Others hear popping or rumbling from the tank, see water under the heater, or notice rusty water at a hot-water faucet.

A cold faucet can point to an element, thermostat, pilot, burner, gas control, or broken dip tube. Popping often comes from sediment at the tank bottom, while a floor leak may come from a valve, fitting, drain connection, or tank seam. These faults can look alike from outside the closet, but they use different parts and require different repair work.

Heater diagnostic callout

We start the callout at the heater cabinet. We read the model plate, inspect the tank shell, check the shutoff valve, look at the drain pan and discharge pipe, and trace visible water around fittings. On electric units, we isolate power before testing terminals, elements, and thermostats. On gas units, we inspect the burner area, pilot or igniter, vent connection, and control valve.

A typical diagnosis takes about thirty to sixty minutes, depending on access and the fault. We can often replace a thermostat, heating element, anode rod, relief valve, or small fitting during the same visit if the part is available. A replacement tank, corroded piping, ordered gas control, or work needing municipal sign-off may need a separate booking.

Tank age and shutoff valves

Tank age affects the repair path. A newer heater with one failed element or thermostat may need a targeted part replacement. A tank with rust at the seam, heavy sediment, or corrosion around the connections can need more extensive work than a single component repair.

Access also changes the work involved. A heater in an open garage is different from a unit behind a washer, inside a narrow closet, or above a finished ceiling. A seized shutoff valve can add piping work, and a model-specific gas control or burner assembly may need to be ordered. Permit or inspection requirements can also affect the scope where a tank replacement is involved.

Model plate and closet access

Check the model plate on the water heater jacket before you call. It usually lists the manufacturer, model number, serial number, fuel type, and tank capacity. Let us know which room holds the heater and whether water is running from the tank, drain pan, relief pipe, or nearby piping.

A photo of the heater closet can show the access path, vent pipe, shutoff valve, and surrounding appliances. If you know the installation age, share that as well. Call TF Plumber Leander at (512) 799-9083 before a tank leak soaks more flooring, drywall, or framing.
