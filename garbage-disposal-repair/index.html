Garbage Disposal Repair in Leander, TX

Garbage disposal repair for humming motors, leaks, jams, and sink backups.

Call (512) 799-9083 Ask a question

Older kitchens in Leander, Cedar Park, Jonestown, and Liberty Hill often have disposal units with worn mounting rings, aging switches, or corroded wiring beneath the sink. A disposal works beside water, food waste, and vibration, so the motor, drain connection, and splash guard all take regular wear.

We check the unit, sink flange, electrical feed, and discharge pipe to find the fault. A humming motor or a leak under the cabinet should not wait, because a jammed motor can overheat and a loose sink connection can damage the cabinet floor.

Humming disposal and sink odor

A disposal that hums without turning is a common call. The grinding plate may be jammed, the reset button may have tripped, or the motor may no longer start. A disposal that makes a rattling sound can have a utensil, bone fragment, or loose internal part inside the grinding chamber.

Sour odor from the sink can come from food trapped around the splash guard, inside the disposal, or in the drain connection. Water under the sink may point to the mounting ring, dishwasher hose, discharge tube, or sink flange. These symptoms can look alike from the counter, but each fault involves different parts and different repair work.

Disposal callout and testing

We start the callout by testing the wall switch, reset button, outlet, and motor response. We check whether the electrical supply reaches the unit at about 120 volts, then inspect the cord, wiring connections, mounting ring, splash guard, and discharge tube.

Initial testing commonly takes 20 to 40 minutes. We remove a jam, clear debris, reset the unit, tighten a mount, or replace a damaged connection during the same visit where the needed part is available. A failed motor, cracked housing, or special mounting assembly may require a replacement unit or part to be booked after the inspection.

We also run water through the sink and disposal to check the drain path and look for leaks at the flange and trap. A standard sink discharge tube is often 1 1/2 inches, but the existing fittings determine what is needed.

Motor age and sink access

The disposal motor and its age affect the repair scope. A unit with a minor jam or worn splash guard needs different work than a unit with a seized motor, damaged grinding chamber, or cracked body.

Access under the sink also matters. Tight cabinets, stored items, corroded mounting hardware, and a seized water shutoff can add work before we can reach the flange or drain connection. A model-specific switch, mounting ring, or dishwasher connector may need to be ordered.

Circuit changes can also affect the scope if the outlet, switch, or wiring needs alteration. Permit or inspection sign-off requirements may apply where electrical work goes beyond the existing disposal connection.

Model number and nameplate

Check the model number on the disposal nameplate before calling. The label is often on the lower housing or side of the unit beneath the sink.

Tell us which kitchen sink has the problem and whether the disposal hums, leaks, drains slowly, or will not turn on. Let us know if water is running from the disposal now and whether cleaning supplies, shelving, or stored items block the cabinet access.

A photo of the mounting area and discharge pipe can help identify the connection. Call TF Plumber Leander at (512) 799-9083 before a leak at the sink flange or disposal body soaks the cabinet base and surrounding wood.
