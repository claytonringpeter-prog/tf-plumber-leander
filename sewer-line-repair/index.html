Sewer Line Repair in Leander, TX

We inspect and repair sewer lines behind backups, odors, and gurgling toilets.

Call (512) 799-9083 Ask a question

People call us saying, 'My toilet bubbles,' 'the tub is filling up,' or 'it smells like sewage outside.' We translate those reports into a check of the sewer line that carries wastewater from the building. A backup may start at one fixture, then show up in another room.

This work should not wait if wastewater is backing up. A clog or damaged pipe can send sewage into tubs, floor drains, or the yard. The problem can also reach more fixtures as the line fills.

Toilets bubbling and sewage backing up

A toilet that gurgles after a shower or laundry cycle is a common report. So is water rising in a tub when a toilet flushes. We also hear about several drains slowing down at once, especially in bathrooms on the same side of the house.

A sewage smell near the yard, garage, or cleanout can point to a cracked pipe or open fitting. Wet ground above a buried line can also be a warning sign. The same symptoms can come from different faults, and each repair has its own labor and material needs.

Sewer camera inspection

We begin at an accessible cleanout or another suitable entry point. We inspect the pipe wall for roots, cracks, separated joints, standing water, and pipe offsets. The camera counter records the distance to a blockage or damaged section.

A camera inspection commonly takes 30 to 60 minutes, depending on the access point and line condition. We can often replace an exposed cleanout cap or a short accessible pipe section during the same visit. Excavation, pipe replacement below concrete, or work needing municipal sign-off is scheduled separately.

Pipe depth, roots, and access

Pipe material changes the repair scope. Clay pipe can have separated joints, while older cast iron can corrode or crack. PVC usually uses different fittings and transition couplings than clay or cast iron.

Depth and route also affect the work. An open yard gives us different access than a line beneath a driveway, patio, slab, or mature tree roots. A buried cleanout, a seized cap, an ordered coupling, or required inspection sign-off can add steps before the repair is complete.

Cleanout location and backup room

Tell us which room has the backup and which fixture first showed the problem. Let us know whether water or sewage is still moving through the tub, toilet, floor drain, or yard. If you know where the cleanout sits, such as beside the house, in the garage, or near a fence, that helps us plan the inspection. Mention any driveway, patio, landscaping, gate, or stored items above the suspected line route. A prior camera report or plumbing map can also help identify the pipe material and past repair location.
